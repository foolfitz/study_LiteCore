## Lessons this stretch

- **The M2-4 control is what turned "a toast in the instrumented run" into "a product toast".** The control's observer records no replies; it shows the same toast and the same duration. Without that pair the toast could have been blamed on the instrument and the shakedown called clean. The pair costs about five minutes per browser.
- **Repetition found what variety did not.** Twenty distinct merges (M2-1) and twenty repeated typed merges (M2-1b) were fast and clean; 105 identical presses on blank paragraphs waited 2 s each and toasted once. My S3 prediction came from typed-merge latencies (729-760 ms), a different population.
- **Un-filtering an output showed what the filtered one hid.** The committed `selftest-output.txt` was grep-filtered; the unfiltered rerun showed the reader exits 0 on a MISS. A self-test's filtered record cannot show how the tool behaves.
- **A mid-chain tool fix has a safe place: after `CHAIN DONE`.** The pack-tool fix waited, was made with its own end-to-end red case, and the make reproduced every hash. Rule m cost nothing here.
- **A new reader may be written after seeing a part's stdout only if the record says so.** `merge/judge.py` was; spec §29.3 and RESULT.md say it, and its expected values come from `PREDICTION.md`. The honest statement is the only thing that keeps that from being a moved goalpost.
- **"Behaviour unchanged" refactors need a run.** `await_delete` was extracted in `e62ddec4`; it has been exercised by the `m2-1b`, `m2-4` and `m2-1-red` runs, not by the default schedule on the new runner.
