# `queue-verify-caret-by-block-identity`, native arm — registered before the probe was written

Written 2026-08-16, before `tools/queue_native_block_identity.cpp` existed and
before any measurement was taken.

## The claim being tested is our own

The queue item says:

> Three separate ambiguities have now been measured and they all reduce to the
> same missing datum — which BLOCK the caret/selection is in […] An engine that
> reported the caret's block index in `editorGetStateV2` would settle all three
> by comparison instead of by tolerance.

That sentence was written when the three ambiguities were found, not after
anyone checked whether such a datum exists or whether it would in fact settle
them. **"It would settle all three" is a prediction, and this round is where it
can fail.**  The three:

1. **finding 046** — `.uno:SelectText` overshoots an empty paragraph, so the
   format barrier verifies the neighbouring paragraph and refuses a mutation
   that succeeded.
2. **finding 052's residual** — a second click outside the text, with the caret
   already clamped there, is indistinguishable from a click the engine swallowed.
   Nothing moves either way.
3. **same line, different x** — `caretIsOnLine` has no x, so a caret sitting on
   the clicked line at a completely different offset is accepted once the grace
   period expires.

## What is already settled by reading core 26.8, and is therefore not measured here

Recorded so the probe is not asked to rediscover it, and so that a reader can
check it without running anything:

* The focused-paragraph payload — shared by `getA11yFocusedParagraph()` and
  `LOK_CALLBACK_A11Y_FOCUS_CHANGED` — carries exactly `content`, `position`,
  `start`, `end`, and `listPrefixLength`
  (`sfx2/source/view/viewsh.cxx`, `paragraphPropertiesToTree`).
  **There is no paragraph index in it.**
* `getCommandValues()` in Writer accepts `.uno:TextFormFields`,
  `.uno:SetDocumentProperties`, `.uno:Bookmarks`, `.uno:Fields`,
  `.uno:Sections`, `.uno:ExtractDocumentStructure` and `.uno:Layout`
  (`sw/source/uibase/uno/loktxdoc.cxx`).  None of them answers "which block is
  the caret in"; `.uno:Layout` returns one `isInvalidContent` flag per page.
* The **notification** is gated on the text changing
  (`if (m_sFocusedParagraph != sText)`), while `m_nCaretPosition` is updated on
  every caret event.  So a synchronous query and the callback do not carry the
  same information, and the probe must use the synchronous one.

So there is no block index to project.  The only per-block datum available today
is the paragraph's **own text**, which is a fingerprint.  Whether a fingerprint
is enough is what the four predictions below are about.

## The fixture

Generated by `tools/create_block_identity_fixture.py` into the run's own output
directory — **not** added to the frozen E1 corpus, whose fixture hashes are
recorded in evidence that this round has no business moving.

```
BI-ANCHOR-ONE                       unique; the search anchor and the control
(empty paragraph)                   finding 046's cell
BI-AFTER-EMPTY                      the paragraph the overshoot reaches
BI-TWIN                             identical pair, first
BI-TWIN                             identical pair, second
BI-LONG …                           long enough to click at two different x
BI-LAST                             the last line; everything below it is blank
```

## Predictions

Each is written so that it can come out false, and each says what its being
false would mean.

* **P-BI-1 (finding 046 is separable by content).**  With the caret on the empty
  paragraph, the synchronous a11y payload reports `content: ""`.  After
  `.uno:DefaultBullet` and the barrier's own selection pair, the readback
  contains text that is **not** in that payload — the text of a neighbouring
  paragraph.  A comparison of the two therefore detects the overshoot.
  *If false*: 046 cannot be settled by this datum and the design must look
  elsewhere.
* **P-BI-2a (finding 052's residual is NOT separable when the click repeats).**
  A first click below the last line changes the focused paragraph to the last
  one.  A **second** click at the **same x**, further down, produces a
  synchronous payload that is **byte-for-byte identical** to the first.
  *If false* — if some field moves — then the residual is separable after all
  and the queue item's "all three" survives as written.
* **P-BI-2b (but it IS separable when x differs).**  A click below the text at a
  **different x** produces a different `position`, because the clamp lands at the
  offset nearest that x in the last line.  Geometry cannot see this — every one
  of these clicks leaves the caret on the same line, which is all
  `caretIsOnLine` compares.
  *If false*: clicks below the text do not carry x into the semantic position,
  and the whole "outside the text" family is unseparable by this datum.
* **P-BI-3 (the third ambiguity is settled by `position`, not by block
  identity).**  Two clicks on the same line of `BI-LONG`, near its start and
  near its end, produce the same `content` and **different `position`**.
  *If false*: either the offset is not reported for clicks, or clicks on one
  line do not distinguish x at the semantic level, and the geometric check
  cannot be replaced by this datum either.
* **P-BI-4 (a11y gives a fingerprint, not an identity).**  With the caret in the
  first `BI-TWIN` and then in the second, the two payloads are byte-for-byte
  identical.
  *If false*: something in the payload distinguishes two paragraphs with the
  same text, and a genuine identity is available without a core change.

## The controls, which are not decoration

* **`control-anchor`** — the caret on a paragraph with unique text.  Without it,
  "every payload is empty" cannot be told from a probe that never enabled
  accessibility.
* **`caretY` on every arm** — P-BI-4 claims two payloads are identical while the
  caret is in two different paragraphs.  Without a rectangle that moves, an
  identical payload would equally mean the caret never went anywhere.
* **`below-control-on-text`** — a click back onto a *different* paragraph after
  the two clicks below the text.  P-BI-2a claims nothing changed; this shows
  that clicks were still being delivered when it claimed it.

## What this round cannot establish, said before it runs

* **Nothing here describes the WASM artifact.**  This is native LibreOffice
  26.8, measured for the same reason finding 048's native arm was: a number
  measured only in the browser cannot separate core's behaviour from our
  transport's.  Whether the shipped engine would see the same payloads is a
  separate question that a relink would have to answer.
* **Where inside core a click "outside the text" is clamped** is not asked here
  and must not be guessed from these payloads.  Findings 040 and 048 are the
  precedents for what guessing costs.
* **Whether a design built on this datum is worth shipping** is not a
  measurement and this round does not decide it.

## Addendum, written after round 1 and before round 2

Round 1 ran and its captures are preserved in `run/`.  Two of its arms did not
test what they were written to test, and the fix is a second round with two more
arms — **not** an edit to anything above, which stays as registered.

1. **P-BI-2b's two x were both at or past the end of the last line.**  The
   fixture's last paragraph is `BI-LAST`, seven characters; the arm clicked at
   the caret x left by the search (offset 7, the end) and again 2400 twips to
   the right of it.  Both clamp to offset 7, so the arm could not have
   distinguished them whatever core did.  Round 1's P-BI-2b result is therefore
   **not evidence about the prediction**, and the judge should say
   NOT_ESTABLISHED rather than FAILED for it — see round 1's README for how it
   was reported before this was noticed.
2. **The finding 046 arm never ran.**  The probe searched for `BI-ANCHOR-ONE` a
   second time to navigate back, and the caret was already there — so no
   `INVALIDATE_VISIBLE_CURSOR` arrived, `searchFor` read that as "not found",
   and the probe exited 9 before the empty-paragraph arm.  *A search that does
   not move the caret is indistinguishable from a search that found nothing*,
   which is worth writing down: it is the same shape as finding 052 one level
   up.  Round 2 navigates via `BI-AFTER-EMPTY` and `.uno:GoUp` instead.

New prediction for round 2, registered here before it runs:

* **P-BI-2c (x separates only inside the line's text).**  A click below the text
  at an x **to the left of the last line's last character** produces a
  `position` lower than the end-of-line offset, while a click at an x past the
  end of that text produces the end-of-line offset no matter how far right it
  is.  So the datum separates "outside the text" clicks **only within the
  horizontal extent of the text**, and finding 052's residual is unseparable
  everywhere else.
  *If false*: either x is not carried into the clamp at all (P-BI-2b's original
  reading), or the clamp does not saturate at the end of the line and every x is
  separable.

## Second addendum, written after round 2 and before round 3

Round 2 ran clean (exit 0) and settled P-BI-2a, P-BI-3 and P-BI-4.  It also
exposed two confounds in the probe and one mistake about the product.  Again:
nothing above is edited, and round 2's captures and verdict stay as they are.

1. **The below-the-text arms could not tell a clamp from a click that did
   nothing.**  Every one of them started with the caret *already* at the end of
   `BI-LAST` — the search that located the line put it there — so "the payload
   did not move" is equally consistent with "the click was never delivered".
   P-BI-2a's HELD in round 2 is true as stated and **weaker than it looks**; the
   round 3 arms below start from a different paragraph every time.
2. **Round 2's readback used the barrier this tree stopped shipping.**
   `.uno:GoToStartOfPara` + `.uno:EndOfParaSel` is the *pre-034* pair; finding
   034 replaced it with `.uno:SelectText`, which is what
   `kFormatBarrierSelectCommand` posts in `probe_engine.cpp`.  So round 2's
   P-BI-1 FAILED is a fact about the old gesture, not about the shipped one.
   What it measured is worth keeping: the read escaped **upward**, to
   `BI-ANCHOR-ONE`, while finding 046's own evidence has it escaping downward on
   a different fixture.  Round 3 posts the shipped command instead.

Registered for round 3, informed by round 2 and written before round 3 runs:

* **P-BI-5 (the click below the text is delivered).**  With the caret on
  `BI-ANCHOR-ONE`, a click below the last line reports `content: "BI-LAST"`.
  *If false*: clicks below the text do nothing at all, and finding 052's
  residual is a different animal from the one the queue item describes.
* **P-BI-6 (x is carried on the line and dropped below it).**  A click **on**
  `BI-LAST`'s own line, 400 twips left of its last character, reports a
  `position` below the end-of-line offset; the same x **below** the text reports
  the end-of-line offset.
  *If false in the first half*: 400 twips is not inside the text and round 2's
  arms were fine after all.  *If false in the second half*: x is carried below
  the text too, and clicks outside the text are separable by this datum.
* **P-BI-7 (the shipped barrier's read escapes as well).**  After
  `.uno:DefaultBullet` on the empty paragraph and then `.uno:SelectText`, the
  readback contains text from a paragraph other than the one the a11y payload
  named at dispatch.
  *If false*: the shipped gesture does **not** overshoot on this fixture, which
  would narrow finding 046 rather than settle it — and would mean the escape
  round 2 saw belongs to the retired pair.

## Third addendum: an adversarial review, and round 4

An external adversarial review (codex, 2026-08-16) attacked the judge and the
probe rather than the conclusions, and was right about six things.  **No
prediction is changed**; the criteria were tightened so that each one tests what
it says it tests, and round 4 re-ran with the arms the tightened criteria need.

| what was wrong | how it was closed |
|---|---|
| **P-BI-5 took the probe's word for it.**  "From a caret on another paragraph" was the arm's name; nothing recorded where the caret was immediately before the click, so a caret already on `BI-LAST` and a click that never arrived produced the same row | the probe emits `before-below-from-elsewhere`, and the judge voids the prediction unless it shows a different paragraph |
| **P-BI-6 never saw a coordinate.**  It compared three offsets while asserting "the same x, on the line and below it" | the probe records `clickX`/`clickY` per arm; the judge voids the prediction unless the two arms share an x and the "on the line" one is above the other |
| **P-BI-7 accepted a whitespace difference as an escape.**  `read_text != dispatch_content` is true of the same paragraph serialised differently | the read must contain `BI-AFTER-EMPTY` — the neighbour, by name |
| **P-BI-3 and P-BI-4 compared `caretY` without reading `caretValid`.**  Two invalid rectangles both report −1 | both arms must have a valid rectangle |
| **P-BI-2c's `separates` half had no mutation**, and neither did P-BI-3's `same_content` half: deleting either clause left the self-test green | one mutation per clause; the self-test is 27 checks |

Also corrected, and it is a correction to this file: the list of Writer
`getCommandValues` commands above is **incomplete**.  It omits the singular
`.uno:TextFormField`, `.uno:Bookmark` and `.uno:Field`, and the generic commands
handled before Writer's dispatcher in `desktop/source/lib/init.cxx`
(`.uno:ReadOnly`, `.uno:LanguageStatus`, `.uno:CharFontName`, `.uno:StyleApply`,
`.uno:Undo`, `.uno:Redo`, `.uno:UndoCount`, `.uno:RedoCount`,
`.uno:AcceptTrackedChanges`, `.uno:TrackedChangeAuthors`,
`.uno:ViewAnnotations`, `.uno:ViewAnnotationsPosition`, `.uno:RulerState`,
`.uno:ViewRenderState`, `.uno:AllPageSize`).  **None of them reports a caret
paragraph index either**, so the conclusion is unchanged — but "the surface was
enumerated" was a stronger claim than what had been read.

Round 4 is round 3's arms plus the two witnesses, judged by the tightened
criteria.  Rounds 1–3 were re-judged by the same criteria and their verdict
files rewritten; round 3's P-BI-5 and P-BI-6 move from HELD to NOT_ESTABLISHED,
because the arms that would establish them did not exist yet.

## How it is judged

Offline, by `tools/analyze_queue_block_identity.py`, which carries its own
self-test: every predicate is flipped and must move the verdict.  The run and
the criteria stay separable, so the criteria cannot be edited into agreement
with what came back.
