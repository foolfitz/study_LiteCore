### Mechanism

1. At a C boundary the engine answers a `delete-backward` only when its window expires: 250 ms for K1's `TEXT_SELECTION`, then one `MergeStepDeadlineMs` (2,000 ms) more, then `verified-boundary-noop` (`rejectSelectionBarrierAtDeadline` → `finishSelectionBarrierBoundaryNoop`, `src/probe_engine.cpp`; SPEC-M3-link-batch §10.5's named cost 「約 2.25 秒」). Measured 2,253 ms in the engine, 2,256 ms on the page, every time, both browsers, both documents, settled or not, early or late after the open.
2. The page sends **every press**: `beforeinput deleteContentBackward` → `editorAction("delete-backward")` → the session queue (`web/e2-editor-app.js` `DELETE_INPUT_TYPES` listener; finding 131's rule "a burst of separate presses is deleted press for press"). The only throttle is H2 (`dropsHeldRepeat`: `event.repeat === true` and two deletes in flight) and finding 134's B (`deleteRefusedThisHold`, after a refusal — C's no-op is not a refusal). The session drains the queue in order and the state stays `busy` until it is empty (`editor-shell/editor-session.js` `_drain`), so N presses cost N × 2.256 s of 「處理中」, with every click or key the user makes meanwhile queued behind them and the toolbar greyed; no toast, no reload, and when the queue empties the page is ready as if nothing happened — which is what the owner described. **About 30 s is 13 presses.**
3. A held key is a burst of presses whenever the keydowns reach Firefox with `repeat === false`. WebDriver and CDP flag repeats (`repeatsFlagged 251` of 255 in 10-14l, 84 of 85 here), so every probe before this one saw H2 cap the hold at depth 2 (≤ 3 no-ops, 6.8 s for a 3 s hold). Keydowns delivered as release/press pairs give one no-op per repeat: 18 in one second, 40.6 s of busy. Thirteen deliberate presses give the same. **The owner says they held Backspace for about 2–3 s, in Firefox, with no VNC viewer** (answers relayed during the task), so the question is what this desktop's input path makes of a held key — see "The owner's input path" below.
4. **Ctrl+Home does not move the document caret.** The page's keydown handler returns on any accelerator before the `KEY_ACTIONS` table (`if (accel) return;`, `web/e2-editor-app.js`), `Home` alone maps to `move-line-home`, and no action in the contract goes to the document start; the chord reaches the hidden textarea, which moves its own caret. A user who goes to the start with Ctrl+Home and presses Backspace deletes a character where the caret really is (measured: `verified-selection-delete`, revision 0 → 1 in 60 ms, the engine's caret x 2129 → 1951 on the first heading's line, the view still 9000 px down until the caret push scrolled it back). Not the 30 s, and not the arm the owner described, but it is on the owner's path to "the document start" and it edits the document silently while the view may be scrolled far from the caret.

### The owner's input path (facts read from this desktop; the path itself was not driven)

- Session: **KDE Plasma 6.6.6 on Wayland** (`XDG_SESSION_TYPE=wayland`, `WAYLAND_DISPLAY=wayland-0`, `XDG_CURRENT_DESKTOP=KDE`; `loginctl` session 3 type wayland on seat0). - `~/.config/kwinrc` `[Wayland] InputMethod=/usr/share/applications/fcitx5-wayland-launcher.desktop`, `VirtualKeyboardEnabled=true`: **KWin starts fcitx5 5.1.19 as its input method** (`fcitx5-wayland-launcher --reopen` and `fcitx5` are running), i.e. fcitx5 holds KWin's input-method keyboard grab and keys for a text-input-enabled surface go **keyboard → KWin → fcitx5 → (if not consumed) KWin's virtual keyboard → Firefox**. Chewing is the configured engine (`~/.config/fcitx5/conf/chewing.conf`).
- Autorepeat settings: no `[Keyboard]` section in `~/.config/kcminputrc` (KWin defaults); XWayland mirrors them as `auto repeat delay: 600, repeat rate: 25` (`xset q` on `:0`, a query). A 2.5 s hold at that rate is one press plus about 47 repeats.
- What that path delivers to Firefox for a held key — whether the repeats arrive as `repeat: true` keydowns (GTK's client-side repeat from `wl_keyboard.repeat_info`, as when no IM grab is involved), as discrete press/release pairs re-emitted through the virtual keyboard (then `repeat: false`, one page request each), or partly dropped — **is not measured here**. Driving it needs real key events into a Wayland client under this compositor with fcitx5 grabbing: no `xdotool`/`ydotool`/`wtype` is installed, `sudo` is not available for uinput, XTEST on `:0` would go to whatever window the owner has focused, and a nested `kwin_wayland --virtual --inputmethod fcitx5` on a private bus with a fake-input client was judged too large for this task. WebDriver's key actions (used here) bypass the compositor and flag repeats, so they cannot stand in for it.
- **The cheapest closing measurement is the owner's**: open `keyrepeat-check.html` (this directory; a dependency-free page, nothing is sent anywhere) in the same Firefox, click the box, hold Backspace for two seconds, and copy its one summary line (keydown count, how many `repeat: true`, median gap, span). The expected shapes: flagged repeats at ≈40 ms → the page's guard applies and a hold costs ≤ 3 no-ops (this task's `hold-3s`: 6.8 s); unflagged keydowns at ≈40 ms → one no-op each (this task's `burst-1s`: 18 → 40.6 s); about 13 unflagged keydowns in 2–3 s → the owner's 30 s exactly.

### Candidates for the 30 s, ranked by what the evidence supports

1. **A held key whose repeats reach Firefox unflagged (`repeat: false`) through KWin's input-method path (fcitx5).** Consistent with: the owner held the key 2–3 s; the measured burst shape (18 unflagged keydowns → 40.6 s, no toast, no reload, generation 1); the session facts (Wayland, fcitx5 as KWin's IM, Firefox a Wayland client). Not consistent yet: at 25 repeats/s a 2–3 s hold would queue 40–60 no-ops (90–135 s), while "about 30 s" is about 13; a slower effective delivery, partial forwarding, or a shorter hold than remembered would close that gap — unmeasured. Status: **inference**, closable by `keyrepeat-check.html`.
2. **Repeated deliberate presses** (a user who sees nothing happen presses again): the measured `double-press` (4.5 s) extrapolates to 13 presses ≈ 30 s; the shape is identical. The owner says "held", not "pressed repeatedly", so this is ranked below 1, but the two are not exclusive (a hold followed by presses).
3. **A held key with flagged repeats**: measured, both browsers, both paths: 85 keydowns → 3 no-ops → 6.8 s. **Refuted** as the sole cause of 30 s.
4. **A 30 s timeout** (`placeCaret`'s 30 s polling exit, the SDK's 30 s default, the drain's `getState` 30 s): no arm showed any request longer than 2,258 ms or any error; the ready time scales with the number of presses (2.26, 4.51, 6.77, 40.6 s), not with a constant. **Refuted** for every sequence measured.
5. The document, the browser, the host, the moment after the open: all measured equal (both of the owner's documents, Chrome = Firefox, dist mirror = host, early = settled).

Whichever of 1 and 2 it was, the product cost is the same mechanism (one queued 2.25 s no-op per press, no feedback), and the fix below does not depend on `event.repeat`.
